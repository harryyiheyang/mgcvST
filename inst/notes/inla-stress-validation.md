# INLA stress and score validation

This report records completed validation evidence. The subsequent implementation applies only to INLA, retains the constrained score statistic, uses Liu calibration without Davies, and uses bounded C++ sparse/OpenMP downstream kernels rather than BiocParallel. The mgcv implementation remains unchanged. The public BiocParallel results below remain historical validation measurements rather than recommendations for the new downstream design.

This validation separates the public 2D implementation from the standalone 3D research calculations. The null study completed all 4,000 planned datasets and produced 8,000 calibration rows, with 500 datasets in each of eight cases. The feature-parallel stress study also completed all 50 full-observation independent fits. The large joint-fit study remains in progress and is reported only as an incomplete execution record below.

The source checkout at launch was `7e80306`, and the executed public package was `mgcvST` 0.0.1.9006. Both the spatial precision and negative-binomial size used flat priors on INLA's internal log scales. An older real-data model object used as input to the public parallel check was generated by package 0.0.1.9005 at source commit `f2ba738`; the actual public fitting and testing in this validation used 0.0.1.9006.

## Design and estimands

The public 2D study is the primary package validation. It crosses pair and marginal nulls with mean counts 0.3 and 3. Each dataset has 200 observations, a 36-node mesh, fixed `kappa=5`, negative-binomial size 2, heterogeneous offsets, and the package-required observation-mean field constraint. Pair datasets contain two independently generated nonzero fields with zero cross-feature association. Marginal datasets contain no spatial field. The public `inlaST` fit and score-test APIs produce both Davies and Liu p-values.

The standalone 3D study is a research calculation. It crosses separate two-gene fits and a 40-gene shared-hyperparameter fit with the same two mean counts. Each dataset uses a genuine 64-node tetrahedral mesh, fixed `kappa=5`, a volume-integral field constraint, and two prespecified tested genes. The 40 generated fields are independent. In the joint model, all 40 fields share one spatial precision and one negative-binomial size, and the other 38 genes inform those common parameters.

The joint and independent 3D fits represent different statistical models. The shared-hyperparameter model agrees with the data-generating assumption in this experiment because the 40 fields were generated with common parameters. It is not a parallelized or accelerated version of 40 independent fits. Comparisons between the two models measure the effect of pooling information under a correct common-parameter assumption.

## Public 2D primary results

The public 2D procedure returned p-values for 495/500 low-count pair datasets, 493/500 low-count marginal datasets, and all 500 datasets in both mean-3 cases. In the unavailable low-count results, the package did not retain a usable NB-size or marginal-score state. These outcomes remain in the denominator bounds; a package-level failure to retain a finite size is distinct from a diagnosed INLA optimization failure.

## Standalone 3D primary and diagnostic results

The original 3D score calculation combines the expected negative-binomial working state with INLA's native conditional intercept variance. This hybrid construction is the primary 3D calculation because it was specified before inspecting the spectrum. Its availability was limited by negative eigenvalues below the implementation's fixed `-1e-10` cutoff.

| 3D model and mean | Calibration | Valid / attempted | Rejections at 0.05 | Rate among valid (exact 95% CI) | All-attempt range |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| independent, 0.3 | Davies | 313 / 500 | 7 | 2.24% (0.90%--4.55%) | 1.4%--38.8% |
| independent, 0.3 | Liu | 313 / 500 | 7 | 2.24% (0.90%--4.55%) | 1.4%--38.8% |
| independent, 3 | Davies or Liu | 9 / 500 | 0 | 0% (0%--33.63%) | 0%--98.2% |
| shared 40-gene, 0.3 | Davies | 396 / 500 | 15 | 3.79% (2.14%--6.17%) | 3.0%--23.8% |
| shared 40-gene, 0.3 | Liu | 396 / 500 | 14 | 3.54% (1.95%--5.86%) | 2.8%--23.6% |
| shared 40-gene, 3 | Davies or Liu | 8 / 500 | 2 | 25.0% (3.19%--65.09%) | 0.4%--98.8% |

The very small valid denominators in the mean-3 cases prevent interpretation of their primary rejection proportions as calibration estimates. The spectrum analysis gives a specific explanation. The native intercept variance follows the observed likelihood curvature closely, whereas the score matrix uses expected curvature. Their mismatch contributes a rank-one subtraction to the score covariance. The volume-integral field constraint does not make the sampled spatial factor orthogonal to the observation intercept, so this subtraction can create negative eigenvalues. At mean 3, the existing cutoff rejected 491/500 independent datasets and 492/500 shared-hyperparameter datasets. Complete matrix identities, eigenvalue scales, and pilot diagnostics are reported in `inst/notes/inla-score-spectrum.md`.

Three alternative routes were evaluated after this mechanism was found: a fixed-working-Gaussian sandwich covariance, an internally consistent expected-curvature calculation, and an internally consistent observed-curvature calculation. These A/B/C routes are post-hoc diagnostics. They neither replace the primary hybrid analysis nor define a new package calibration rule.

The expected-curvature results provide a concise view of the sensitivity calculation:

| 3D model and mean | Calibration | Valid / attempted | Rejections at 0.05 | Rate (exact 95% CI) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| independent, 0.3 | Davies | 496 / 500 | 12 | 2.42% (1.26%--4.19%) |
| independent, 0.3 | Liu | 496 / 500 | 12 | 2.42% (1.26%--4.19%) |
| independent, 3 | Davies | 500 / 500 | 23 | 4.60% (2.94%--6.82%) |
| independent, 3 | Liu | 500 / 500 | 20 | 4.00% (2.46%--6.11%) |
| shared 40-gene, 0.3 | Davies | 500 / 500 | 21 | 4.20% (2.62%--6.35%) |
| shared 40-gene, 0.3 | Liu | 500 / 500 | 20 | 4.00% (2.46%--6.11%) |
| shared 40-gene, 3 | Davies or Liu | 500 / 500 | 27 | 5.40% (3.59%--7.76%) |

The expected, observed, and sandwich routes produced broadly similar rejection frequencies. This supports the diagnosed curvature mechanism and shows that consistent score constructions remain computationally evaluable. The post-hoc agreement is evidence for further method development rather than retrospective validation of the hybrid primary rule.

## Public feature parallelism

The public `BPPARAM` check used the same ten features and 45 pair tests under serial, two-worker SOCK, and four-worker SOCK execution. Each worker loaded package 0.0.1.9006, and INLA used one internal thread. All parallel feature fits converged and every pair remained available.

| Configuration | Fit time (s) | Pair-test time (s) | Maximum pair p-value difference from serial |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| serial | 19.01 | 3.09 | reference |
| SOCK, 2 workers | 14.02 | 4.28 | 4.72e-6 |
| SOCK, 4 workers | 12.21 | 5.19 | 1.97e-5 |

Feature fitting benefited from parallel workers on this ten-feature input, while pair testing was slower because the fixed set of 45 small tests did not amortize worker overhead. The fitted fields and test results remained numerically close to serial execution. This is the public 2D parallel result; it does not establish 3D score scalability.

One feature, `ENSDARG00000001431`, had an NB-size estimate close to the Poisson boundary. Its size estimates were approximately 1.13e19, 5.57e6, and 7.73e16 under serial, two-worker, and four-worker execution. The two-worker fit emitted INLA non-informativeness and skewness warnings. All original warnings and estimates are retained; similar spatial states and pair p-values do not imply stable size estimation at that boundary.

## Full-observation fitting stress

The independent fitting stress used 97,830 observations, 1,962 field nodes, ten fixed genes, and the same response arrays in five worker/thread configurations. All 50 fits completed with `mode_status=0`, zero warnings, finite modes, and maximum constraint errors below `7.63e-13`.

| Workers x INLA threads | Ten-fit wall time (s) | Throughput (fits/min) | Peak summed private memory (GiB) | Minimum system memory available (GiB) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 x 1 | 154.81 | 3.88 | 0.85 | 23.29 |
| 2 x 1 | 72.72 | 8.25 | 1.69 | 21.12 |
| 4 x 1 | 51.53 | 11.64 | 3.34 | 20.54 |
| 8 x 1 | 37.72 | 15.91 | 6.62 | 19.16 |
| 4 x 2 | 43.12 | 13.91 | 3.40 | 19.17 |

Eight single-thread workers gave the highest observed throughput. Scaling was sublinear because individual elapsed times increased under concurrent load. Single-thread configurations reproduced field and hyperparameter modes to numerical optimization tolerance. The two-thread fits showed slightly larger numerical variation, with maximum within-gene ranges of 0.0161 for negative-binomial size and 0.000442 for field standard deviation across all configurations.

The full 97,830-observation stress test measures fitting only. It does not calculate a full 3D score matrix. The current standalone 3D score experiment uses a dense 200 by 200 observation covariance, so the completed fitting stress cannot be cited as evidence that the current full-observation 3D score calculation scales. A coefficient-space research route can avoid an n × n matrix and retain dense q × q quantities at q=1,962, but that route first requires an equality check against the existing small dense calculation.

## Joint fitting stress: incomplete

The requested joint workload comprises ten repeated 40-gene fits at 5,000 observations per gene and ten at 97,830 observations per gene, each with four INLA threads. At the report snapshot, the 5,000-observation group was still running and the full-observation group had not started. This section is intentionally incomplete. Final completion counts, failure or resource-stop status, wall times, and process-tree memory will be added from the supervisor records after the run ends.

The completed native-replicate pilot is separate from this workload. On three genes and 500 observations per gene, explicit block `generic0` took 35.97 s and native `replicate` took 40.32 s. Both fits converged without INLA warnings, their field means correlated at 0.9999999, and their marginal log likelihoods differed by 0.00233. The pilot establishes feasibility of the replicated constrained representation, but it provides no speed advantage and does not replace the explicit 40-gene stress model.
